## 0. How to read this dossier

**Polarity.** P6-I1 to P6-I4 record the **presence** of restrictions (a restriction found = evidence for a positive score). **P6-I5 is inverse**: the rows record the binding data-transfer agreements that **exist**, which support a score of **0**.

**Scope exclusion (whole pillar).** Measures applying **only to government data** are recorded but **not scored** — flagged `[GOV-ONLY]`.

**Quote labels.**
- `[OFFICIAL-EN]` — verbatim from an authentic or officially-published English text.
- `[OWN-TRANSLATION]` — my English rendering of the authentic Thai / Hindi / Bahasa Indonesia text.

> **Material provenance warning added after adversarial verification.** The four Indian Department of Telecommunications instruments of June–July 2026 are published in the Gazette **in Devanagari only**, with English marginal headings. An earlier pass produced fluent "English gazette quotes" for these instruments; a targeted re-fetch established that **no English body text exists in those PDFs**. |---|---|---|---|---|---|

**Three things a coder must not miss.**
1. **India's DPDP transfer architecture is NOT in force in August 2026.** Act s.16 and DPDP Rules 2025 rr. 13(4) and 15 all commence **13 May 2027**; the s.16(1) restricted-country list has never been notified. India's operative Pillar-6 weight sits in sectoral regulators and the June–July 2026 DoT rules. *(Commencement dates below are Medium confidence — see §5.)*
2. **Indonesia has no horizontal private-sector localisation.** PP 71/2019 Art. 21 is *permissive* for private ESOs; it abolished PP 82/2012's blanket rule. All real localisation flows from the delegation in **PP 71/2019 Art. 21(4)** to OJK and Bank Indonesia. An assessor reading only PP 71/2019 will wrongly score Indonesia at zero.
3. **Thailand's PDPA regime is structurally the opposite of a ban** — s.28 adequacy plus derogations, s.29 BCRs / SCCs / ASEAN MCCs / certification, and from 17 February 2026 an operational BCR certification procedure. Trap 1 disposes of any 6.1 reading.

## 4. Negative findings

### 4.1 Thailand
1. **No P6-I1 ban found.** Outside the already-held credit-information rules, no Thai instrument prohibits cross-border transfer outright or mandates domestic-only processing with no transfer path. The PDPA regime is structurally the opposite — s.28 (adequacy + derogations) and s.29 (BCRs, SCCs/ASEAN MCCs, certification, intergovernmental instruments) create multiple exit routes. Trap 1 disposes of any 6.1 reading.
2. **No horizontal local-storage-copy requirement (6.2).** Nothing in the PDPA, the Cybersecurity Act B.E. 2562, the Computer Crime Act framework, the Electronic Transactions Act or the Royal Decree on Digital Platform Services requires a copy of data to be kept in Thailand. The only local-retention duties found (TH-08, TH-09) are implicit rather than express.
3. **No general infrastructure precondition (6.3).** No Thai law requires a local server, data centre or facility as a condition of providing a service to Thai users. The single in-Kingdom asset clause found (TH-07) is classified-government-meeting-only and appears not carried into v2.0.
4. **No PDPC adequacy list published.** The s.28 notification empowers the PDPC to designate adequate countries or international organisations by list or case-by-case; no adequacy list or country designation was found as at August 2026. *(Caveat: pdpc.or.th could not be reached, so this is partly absence-of-access — see §5.)*
5. **BOT payment-systems rulebook contains no data residency.** The consolidated BOT payment-oversight rulebook (สนช. 6/2561 / สกช. 4/2566), clause 4.2.3(5.4), requires only risk assessment and contingency planning where data storage/processing is outsourced abroad — expressly no requirement that data or servers sit in Thailand. https://www.bot.or.th/content/dam/bot/documents/th/our-roles/payment-systems/payment/ประมวลกำกับบริการ_29.6.66.pdf
6. **BOT licensing rules for designated payment services** (Notification สนช. 5/2561, 16 Apr 2018) contain no localisation or offshore-approval requirement — only IT-security policy and outsourcing-description filings at application stage. https://www.bot.or.th/content/dam/bot/fipcs/documents/FPG/2561/EngPDF/25610088.pdf
7. **BOT IT-risk rulebook สกช. 5/2566** (repealing สนส. 21/2562) contains **no** data-residency clause, no Thai-data-centre requirement and no BOT approval requirement for sending data abroad; its content is security-control material → trap 2, excluded as **P7-I2**. https://www.bot.or.th/content/dam/bot/fipcs/documents/FOG/2566/ThaiPDF/25660202.pdf
8. **BOT Data Governance policy guideline** ธปท.ฝนส2.ว. 914/2564 (27 Sep 2021) covers third-party/business-partner data governance but contains no offshore-approval or storage-location rule. https://www.bot.or.th/content/dam/bot/fipcs/documents/FPG/2564/ThaiPDF/25640163.pdf
9. **OIC e-insurance notification B.E. 2560** (22 Feb 2017) contains no requirement that servers, systems or data be located in Thailand and no restriction on sending data abroad. https://oiceservice.oic.or.th/document/File/Law/175/3bfb2729-781e-47ef-b954-3cee2d74225c.E.%202560.pdf
10. **NBTC.** The current telecom consumer-data instrument — ประกาศ กสทช. เรื่อง มาตรการคุ้มครองสิทธิของผู้ใช้บริการโทรคมนาคมเกี่ยวกับข้อมูลส่วนบุคคล สิทธิในความเป็นส่วนตัว และเสรีภาพในการสื่อสารถึงกันโดยทางโทรคมนาคม พ.ศ. 2566 (gazetted 17 Aug 2023) — imposes purpose limitation and separated consent (ข้อ 6), security measures (ข้อ 14) and interception prohibitions (ข้อ 15), but **no cross-border transfer clause and no in-Thailand retention clause** was identified. *Not verified against the gazette text.*
11. **Royal Decree on Digital Platform Services B.E. 2565 (2022):** no data-localisation or cross-border duty. Its s.20 information-provision duty runs to **ETDA, a domestic authority** → trap 3, not a cross-border flow. The implementing suite (ETDA DPS 1–6/2566, 2/2567, 1/2568, 4 & 6/2568, 2/2569; Electronic Transactions Commission notifications of 29 Jun 2566, 7 Aug 2566 and 2566) concerns notification forms, AMAU calculation, terms-and-conditions publication, user remedies, marks and fee transparency — **none touches data storage, retention location or transfer abroad**. https://www.etda.or.th/th/regulator/Digitalplatform/law.aspx
12. **Cybersecurity Act B.E. 2562:** no cross-border or localisation provision identified; its CII regime is incident-reporting and security-standards material → **P7**, excluded here.
13. **No data-centre licensing regime identified** in Thailand → no `[OUT-OF-SCOPE-9.4]` flag raised. This is a "none found", not an exhaustive negative.
14. **No rules governing already-established data centres** were found that would need recording at zero.
15. **Business-transfer trap:** no Thai instrument encountered treats transfer of a business, undertaking, assets or shares as a data transfer — no false positives to exclude on that ground.

### 4.2 India
1. **Telecommunications (Telecom Cyber Security) Rules, 2024** · G.S.R. 520(E) · 28 Aug 2024 — no storage-in-India, no transfer restriction, no server-location mandate; rule 3(3) is an unauthorised-access safeguard only. *Notable*: the DoT localisation mandate arrived not through the cyber-security rules but through the 2026 **authorisation** rules (IN-01 to IN-03).
2. **Aadhaar (Data Security) Regulations, 2016** (consolidated to 15 Feb 2024) — no geographic restriction on storage or transfer; information-security/audit/confidentiality only → **P7-I2**, excluded.
3. **RBI (Digital Payment Security Controls) Directions, 2021** · RBI/2020-21/74 · 18 Feb 2021 — no independent localisation; para 8(viii) defers to "extant laws/instructions"; encryption/access-control content → **P7-I2**, excluded.
4. **Master Direction – NBFC – Account Aggregator (Reserve Bank) Directions, 2016** (updated 6 Sep 2024) — no localisation clause. Structurally stricter in a different way: no financial information accessed from FIPs may **reside with** the AA at all, so the question of *where* it is stored does not arise.
5. **Guidelines for Managing Risk in Outsourcing of Financial Services by Co-operative Banks** · RBI/2021-22/64 · 28 June 2021 — no offshore chapter, no records-in-India requirement; s.5.3 country risk is assessment-only.
6. **DoT Other Service Provider (OSP) framework** (guidelines of 5 Nov 2020, revised 23 June 2021; BPO/ITeS) — data-localisation and related restrictions were **removed** in the 2020–21 liberalisation. The surviving requirement is domestic retention of CDR/UDR/system logs for furnishing to DoT/security agencies, and "No restriction for data interconnectivity between any OSP centers." A **liberalising** move, not since reversed.
7. **GST e-invoicing under rule 48(4), CGST Rules 2017 / Invoice Registration Portal** — imposes IRN generation through a government portal; no localisation obligation on taxpayers and no cross-border flow restriction. Government-system architecture, not a flow measure.
8. **DPDP Act 2023, s.16(1) restricted-country list — not notified.** No instrument exists to cite.
9. **DPDP Rules 2025, rule 13(4) "specified personal data" — the specification has not been made**, so the clause has no operative scope even prospectively until it issues.
10. **RBI IT Outsourcing Directions 2023, Appendix I para 5** (cloud provider selection) — contractual enforceability/confidentiality, not a facility mandate → excluded from I3.
11. **No Indian data-centre licensing regime found** → no `[OUT-OF-SCOPE-9.4]` flag raised. (The DoT "Digital Connectivity Infrastructure Provider" authorisation is a telecom-network authorisation, not DC licensing, and is captured at IN-01.)
12. **No rules on already-established data centres** found separately from the above.

1. **No P6-I1 finding — score 0.** Every domestic-processing mandate located has an express escape valve, which under trap 1 converts it to 6.4. The three strongest near-misses, recorded at tier 0 so the reasoning is auditable:

3. **PMK 196/PMK.03/2007** — governs foreign-language/foreign-currency bookkeeping only; **no** territorial storage rule. The tax localisation is in the KUP Law itself (ID-01). *Correcting a common mis-citation.*

8. **PP 80/2019 Pasal 25 ayat (1)** — 10-year retention for financial-transaction PMSE data, 5-year for non-financial, but **no territorial element**. **Scored 0; contrast with KUP Pasal 28(11) (ID-01), which does have one.** Flagged deliberately as an easy false positive for 6.2.

### The "binding?" judgement call, stated openly

**RCEP Chapter 12 is the weakest form of a "binding" commitment**, on three cumulative defects: (a) **total DS exclusion** (Art. 12.17.3 removes the whole chapter from Chapter 19 DS; DS applies only after a general review under Art. 20.8 and then only "between those Parties that have agreed to its application"); (b) a **self-judging security exception** ("any measure that it considers necessary… Such measures shall not be disputed by other Parties"); and (c) a **self-judging public-policy exception** (footnote: "the necessity behind the implementation of such legitimate public policy shall be decided by the implementing Party"). Combined, a Party may lawfully derogate on its own unreviewable determination and no other Party can litigate it.

**Recommended coding:** the text states a binding discipline, so on a strict textual reading of P6-I5 it supports score 0 — **but flag it as not hard-binding and not enforceable.** If ESCAP's methodology requires an *enforceable* commitment, RCEP alone would not settle the matter for Thailand or Indonesia. What genuinely drives score 0 is **AANZFTA Ch. 10 Arts. 17/18 for Thailand** (DS suspended, not excluded; attaches 21 Apr 2028) and **IA-CEPA Art. 13.11.2 for Indonesia** (no DS carve-out at all — an ordinary enforceable treaty obligation from 5 July 2020).

**For India, no instrument clears even the low bar.** India–UAE CEPA fails on the verb, the qualifier and the DS carve-out; India–UK CETA has no substantive data-flow article at all, only a forward-review trigger. TEPA and ECTA have no digital chapter. India is not in RCEP, CPTPP, DEPA, the WTO JSI or Convention 108. **P6-I5 = 1 for India is supportable**, subject to the verification caveats in §5.
