# Pillar 2 deep research: Russian Federation (public procurement of ICT goods and digital services)

**As-of:** measures in force as of **August 2026**. Research done **29 September 2026**.
Consolidated texts were read from the official ИПС «Законодательство России» (pravo.gov.ru) print views. They are consolidated "по состоянию на 29.09.2026". Amendments dated September 2026 are flagged where the text shows them.
**Method note:** all quotes are my own translations from the official Russian text [OWN-TRANSLATION] and are 25 English words or fewer. Russian instruments are cited by their ИПС `nd` document number and, where one exists, by their official-publication number on publication.pravo.gov.ru. Garant, ConsultantPlus, cntd and cis-legislation were not used as evidence.

## Scoring summary

**Proposed scores: P2-I1 (2.1) = 1 · P2-I2 (2.2) = 1 (Medium; fallback 0.5) · P2-I3 (2.3) = 1 · P2-I4 (2.4) = 1.**

- **2.1 = 1.** The 1 January 2025 national-regime overhaul (Federal Law 318-FZ + Government Resolution 1875) contains several full exclusions that follow ESCAP's "foreigners admitted only if no domestic supply" pattern:
  - a ban on foreign software, including cloud/SaaS and development services that transfer rights (Annex 1 pos. 146). It may be lifted only if the Russian/EAEU software registers hold no software of the same class (para 5(е)–(ж));
  - a ban on foreign integrated circuits and smart cards (Annex 1 pos. 25–26). It may be lifted only by a Minpromtorg "no Russian production" permit;
  - the «второй лишний» rule for computers and telecom equipment (Annex 2 pos. 191–242): every foreign bid is rejected once one conforming Russian/EAEU bid exists;
  - Decree 166 / Resolution 1478 for state-owned 223-FZ customers' critical-infrastructure (CII) software.

  Group-specific exclusions (offshore companies; the Decree 250 ban on security tools from "unfriendly" states) would add 0.5 each, but they are not needed.
- **2.2 = 1 (Medium confidence).** The brief's rule scores a *mandatory* vesting of source code or IP in the government as 1. Russian law does this:
  - Civil Code Art. 1240¹(4)(1) vests in the state the IP in budget-funded contract results that are needed for state services or functions;
  - GosTech Regulation para 11 vests in the state all software built on GosTech for state information systems;
  - the National Fund of Algorithms and Programs (NFAP) Regulation requires the distribution and source code of such programs to be deposited.

  None of these is a *pre-bid* condition, and ESCAP may treat commissioned-work vesting as normal practice. If so, the fallback is **0.5**: every 44-FZ bid must carry a qualified e-signature made with FSB-conformity-confirmed (GOST) means (44-FZ Art. 5(1) + 63-FZ Art. 5(4)). The held gold scored 2.2 = 0 on 223-FZ alone.
- **2.3 = 1.** Several limitations discriminate by origin or nationality:
  - the statutory 15% price advantage for all-Russian/EAEU goods (44-FZ Art. 14(4)(3); 223-FZ Art. 3¹⁻⁴(4)(3); 1875 para 1);
  - a 25% small-business set-aside where SME status requires Russian registration and at most 49% foreign corporate ownership (44-FZ Art. 30 + 209-FZ Art. 4);
  - origin-only reference pricing (1875 para 7(в));
  - minimum Russian-goods shares for state-controlled 223-FZ companies (Annex 3, e.g. laptops 80%);
  - ownership- and citizenship-based origin tests for software (149-FZ Art. 12¹; the 2026 "trusted software" list) and for radio-electronics (Resolution 719 Section IX).

  On the transparency limb, the laws themselves are public. However, statutory and regulatory carve-outs since 2022 let sanctioned customers buy through closed procedures and withhold supplier data (44-FZ Art. 24(11)(5)(б), Art. 103(5)(2); 223-FZ Art. 4(16) + Resolution 301). I record these but do not rely on them for the score.
- **2.4 = 1.** WTO lists Russia as a GPA **observer since 29 May 2013**, marked "Pending GPA accession negotiations". Russia is not a party, so it has no CPC 752/754/84 coverage. EAEU Treaty Section XXII / Annex 25 is preferential context only.

Weighted pillar result (40/32/20/8): **1.00** with 2.2 = 1, or **0.84** with the 2.2 fallback of 0.5.

## Gold re-mapping notes

1. **Gold 2.1 = 1 (44-FZ Art. 14).**
   - Art. 14 parts 1–3 are an *enabling* clause (legal-basis trap → 0 by itself). Since 318-FZ, parts 4–5 do contain self-executing rejection rules, but these operate only once the Government adopts a measure.
   - The score of 1 is correct, but it should rest on the implementing instrument: **1875 Annex 1 pos. 146 + para 5(е)–(ж)** (software ban lifted only if no domestic analogue exists) and **Annex 2 pos. 191–242 + Art. 14(4)(2)(а)** («второй лишний»).
   - The gold's Art. 14 text should be dated to the **318-FZ of 08.08.2024** wording (applicable from 01.01.2025), not "December 2024".
2. **Gold 2.1 = 0.5 (149-FZ Art. 10 parts 8–10, foreign-messenger ban).**
   - Part 8 bans the *use* of listed foreign messengers by state bodies, state companies, majority-state companies, banks and others when providing services. It concerns use, not procurement.
   - The ESCAP FAQ allows a government-only ban on a foreign app to be recorded under 2.1 with [ALSO-10.1]. But the bound entities include private credit organisations, so the rule is only partly government-only.
   - Recommendation: keep it as a supporting 0.5 [ALSO-10.1] at most. It does not change 2.1, which is 1 on the software and ICT exclusions.
3. **Gold 2.3 = 1 (1875 cl. 3).**
   - Para 3 only sets the *evidence* of origin (register numbers). The discriminatory measures are para 1 (ban, restriction, advantage), para 2 + Annex 3 (minimum share) and the 15% advantage in 44-FZ Art. 14(4)(3).
   - Recommendation: re-pinpoint the row to para 1 abz. 4 / Art. 14(4)(3). Its Annex 2 «второй лишний» element belongs under 2.1 by the FAQ pattern.
4. **Gold 2.3 = 1 (Res. 878 cl. 3 as amended by Res. 486).**
   - **Superseded.** Para 3 and paras 3¹–3³ were repealed by Res. 1875 from 01.01.2025 (1875 repeal list item 18; the ИПС text shows "утратил силу").
   - In substance, "reject all foreign bids if one EAEU bid is received" is an *exclusion* (2.1 = 1 under the FAQ), not a 2.3 limitation. The three-tier logic now lives in 1875 Annex 2 pos. 195, 197–199 and 203 + para 4(т) + para 7(б).
   - Recommendation: retire the row, or convert it into a currentness row, and re-map the successor to 2.1.
5. **Gold 2.3 = 1 (Res. 1442 + Res. 1352 paras 5.2–5.3).**
   - The nationality element comes from the SME status test (209-FZ requires Russian registration and caps foreign ownership at 49%). The core quota paragraphs are 1352 paras 5 and 5¹ (≥25% / ≥20%), now added as a new row.
   - The 1442 "innovative and high-tech products" volume is not nationality-based; on its own it would be at most 0.5.
6. **Gold 2.2 = 0 (223-FZ; Res. 313 / FSB Order 66 as context).**
   - Applying the brief's 2.2 condition trap: Civil Code Art. 1240¹(4)(1) and GosTech Regulation para 11 *mandatorily* vest software IP in the State, and the NFAP requires deposit of source code. That fits the brief's "mandatory … vesting of source code/IP in the government" → **1 (Medium)**.
   - If ESCAP treats vesting of commissioned work as ordinary practice, the fallback is **0.5**: the qualified e-signature with FSB-certified GOST means required to bid.
   - Res. 313 and FSB Order 66 (crypto licensing) remain [OUT-OF-SCOPE-11.4]. Both are in force per the ИПС; Res. 313 was last amended 28.08.2026 No. 1103, content not reviewed.
7. **Brief corrections.**
   - 318-FZ is dated **08.08.2024**, not 29.10.2024.
   - Annex 3 of 1875 is the **minimum-share** list, not the 15% advantage. The advantage is set by para 1 abz. 4 and the statute.
   - Resolution 1478 of **22.08.2022** (CII software) is verified. Do not confuse it with a different Res. 1478 of 27.09.2025 that amends 1875.
   - FSTEC Order 17 is **repealed** from 01.03.2026 and replaced by Order 117.

## Negative findings

Reference law for all rows: 44-FZ (http://pravo.gov.ru/proxy/ips/?docbody=&nd=102164547) and 223-FZ (http://pravo.gov.ru/proxy/ips/?docbody=&nd=102149420).

- **2.1/2.3 – services by foreign persons (non-software).** Res. 1875 para 1 abz. 4 gives the 15% advantage to **goods** only. Outside Annex 1 pos. 146 (software-related services), pos. 147–151 (audit and finance, non-ICT) and the defence/security customers under para 4(ж), no ban, restriction or preference applies to IT or telecom *services* supplied by foreign persons. The statutory basis exists but is unused (44-FZ Art. 14 part 5; legal-basis trap → 0).
- **2.2 – no source-code escrow as a bid condition.** 44-FZ Arts. 31 and 33, 223-FZ Art. 3, and Res. 1875 impose no requirement to hand over source code, patents or trade secrets in order to bid or to win for commercial off-the-shelf software or hardware. Register criteria (1236 para 5(и)) require code storage in Russia by the right-holder, not surrender.
- **2.2 – 44-FZ Art. 31 part 1 point 8.** This requires the bidder to *own* exclusive IP rights where the customer will acquire rights under the contract. It is a capability requirement, not a surrender condition → 0.
- **2.2 – FSTEC Order 117 / former Order 17.** These are security rules binding the operating agency, and the contractor terms-of-reference clause is optional → 0 [OUT-OF-SCOPE-11.4].
- **2.2 – FSB-certified СКЗИ as a general tender condition.** No 44-FZ, 223-FZ or 1875 provision makes FSB-certified cryptographic tools a general bid-eligibility criterion for ICT tenders. Such requirements enter only through customer specifications derived from agency security rules (FSTEC 117 para 7; FSB rules under 149-FZ Art. 16(5)) → 0. Licensing under Res. 313 / FSB Order 66 → [OUT-OF-SCOPE-11.4].
- **2.2 – NFAP (Res. 62).** Depositing source code is the agency's duty. The contractor deposits only if the contract provides for it (para 19) → 0 on its own.
- **2.3 – consultants.** 44-FZ Art. 31 part 7 requires bidder requirements to apply equally to all participants. No general nationality requirement for consultants was found, apart from 1875 pos. 147–151, which is non-ICT.
- **2.3 – Res. 1442 (innovative products).** The volume target is not nationality-based → 0 as a nationality test (0.5 at most as a status-based volume).
- **2.4.** Russia is not a party to the GPA (see table).
