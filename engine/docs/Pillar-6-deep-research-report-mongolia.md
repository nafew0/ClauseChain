## Negative findings

P6-I1 — Ban/local processing: score 0. I found no in-force horizontal or sectoral rule that prohibits cross-border transfer outright with no lawful transfer path. Most importantly, the apparent prohibition in Personal Information Law Article 14.1 is followed in the same sentence by three routes—law, Mongolia's international treaties, or data-subject consent—so under your trap it is necessarily I4, not I1.https://legalinfo.mn/mn/detail?lawId=16390288615991 The proposed 2026 Data Bill does not change the current score: Article 11.6 has an approval path and the bill was only submitted to Parliament on 24 August. https://legalinfo.mn/mn/discussiondetail/17437287308132?page=1A&type=LIS_LAW

P6-I2 — Local-storage copy: score 0. I found no in-force measure that requires keeping a domestic copy while permitting the operative/primary data to remain offshore. The strongest financial-sector candidate, Bank of Mongolia Order A-231, instead requires both the primary and backup credit-information centres/servers to be in Mongolia. Under your classification rule, that is I3 infrastructure localisation, not I2. https://legalinfo.mn/mn/detail?lawId=211161

The Payment System Procedure, Bank of Mongolia Order A-91 of 10 May 2023, was specifically checked rather than inferred from foreign analogues. The current rules impose main/backup IT-system, database, availability and risk-management requirements, but I found no provision requiring those payment databases or servers to be geographically located in Mongolia. A merchant being permitted to accept payments only at agreed locations in Mongolia is a service/merchant-location rule, not data localisation.https://legalinfo.mn/mn/detail?lawId=16231238887911&type=3&utm_source=chatgpt.com Accordingly it supplies no I1/I2/I3 row.

The 2023 sensitive-data rule contains many security duties—licensed software, audits, SSL, firewalling, backups—but I scored only clauses 3.2.1–3.2.2's actual geographic server/access requirement. This implements your security-rulebook trap and avoids importing P7-I2 measures into Pillar 6. https://legalinfo.mn/mn/detail?lawId=16760452348261

Likewise, searches of the current insurance/financial regulatory material did not uncover an insurance-record analogue requiring Mongolian territorial storage. Rules concerning transfer or amalgamation of an insurance business, opening a foreign branch, assets or licences were excluded because they are business transfers, not cross-border transfers of data. The same exclusion was applied to domestic-regulator access/reporting obligations. https://legalinfo.mn/mn/detail?lawId=100947

For P6-I4 subordinate implementation, I could not locate a separate in-force Mongolian adequacy list, BCR mechanism, SCC notification or detailed foreign-transfer approval regulation implementing Article 14.1 through 26 August 2026. This aligns with ESCAP's 2025 assessment that detailed cross-border-transfer procedures were absent and with its recommendation that Mongolia issue interim transfer guidelines.

The official June 2026 ESCAP economy profile independently confirms the most important I3 measure: the September 2023 regulation requires servers processing sensitive personal information to be physically in Mongolia and accessible exclusively from Mongolia.

I could not verify any additional operator-specific, confidential or unpublished telecom/banking/financial licence condition imposing cross-border-data localisation. Such a condition may conceivably exist in an individual licence file that is not published, but it cannot responsibly be scored without an official public instrument.

I also could not find an official English translation for A/90, A-231, FRC No.995, CRC No.05, CRC No.242/56, A/64, or the 2026 Data Bill. Therefore their table quotations are explicitly marked [OWN-TRANSLATION] rather than being presented as official English.

This is an explicit negative finding, not an inference that such mechanisms could never be adopted later.

P6-I5 conclusion: 1.0. Mongolia has international instruments touching digital trade, electronic documents or personal-data transfers, but I found no treaty in force for Mongolia containing the hard, enforceable free-flow discipline demanded by RDTII 2.1. Japan–Mongolia is explicitly soft on flows; the UN paperless agreement uses “shall endeavour” for exchange; the July 2026 EAEU agreement imposes privacy safeguards rather than a right to transfer; and Mongolia is not party to RCEP, DEPA or Convention 108.
