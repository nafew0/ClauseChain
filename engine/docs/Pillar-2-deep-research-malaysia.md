# Pillar 2 deep research: Malaysia (public procurement of ICT goods and digital services)

**Malaysia · Pillar 2 · in force as of August 2026 · researched 29 Sep 2026**

*Method.* I read every provision cited below on an official portal during this session. The portals I reached were mof.gov.my (AP 2023 PDF), ppp.treasury.gov.my (the Treasury's "Portal Pekeliling Perbendaharaan", which replaced 1pp.treasury.gov.my; I downloaded 33 PK circulars and MOF circular letters), lom.agc.gov.my (Acts 61, 88, 120, 854 and 882, and P.U.(A) 221/2024), parlimen.gov.my, jdn.gov.my / dasar.jdn.gov.my, nacsa.gov.my, cybersecurity.my, mcmc.gov.my, fta.miti.gov.my / miti.gov.my, wto.org, and mfat.govt.nz for the AANZFTA treaty text. Malay text is quoted in my own translation.
*Blocked.* eperolehan.gov.my returned HTTP 403 on every page and PDF, and WebFetch also got 403. myprocurement.treasury.gov.my refused the connection or timed out. federalgazette.agc.gov.my failed DNS, so I used LOM's gazette copies instead. 1pp.treasury.gov.my failed DNS (it is superseded by ppp.treasury.gov.my). dfat.gov.au timed out.

## 3. Negative findings

Reference general procurement law for all of these: AP 166.1 and 166.1A (the Treasury is the central procurement authority; procurement methods are set by PP/PK), PK 1.1 paras 3–5 (legal basis and the open-competition principle), and FPA s. 4.

1. **No government-only ban on a named foreign vendor, app or cloud provider** ([ALSO-10.1] test). I found none in the PPP PK index (all PK titles checked 29 Sep 2026), the JDN in-force policy compilation "Kompilasi Dokumen Dasar JDN Bil. 1/2026" (to 30 Jun 2026), or NACSA's pages. The Cloud Framework Agreement includes AWS, Microsoft and Google (PK 2.6 para 2.1). Reference: PK 1.1 para 5.1(iv) (open competition).
2. **No general local-agent requirement for foreign suppliers.** PK 2.2 para 3.1 bars agencies from imposing participation conditions beyond para 2.5 (registration). PK 2.2 para 6.1(vi) *permits* agents of FTA-country firms at site visits. PK 7.6 para 1.2(iii) allows the software owner *or* a sole agent. The local-presence burden that does exist is SSM and MOF registration (R31). Reference: PK 2.2 paras 2.5, 3.1.
3. **No source-code escrow or disclosure of the supplier's pre-existing source code or patents as a tender condition**, beyond the vesting clauses R19 and R20. Checked: PK 2.1, 2.2 (including Lampiran 2), 2.8, 2.9, 7.6; JDN SPA Bil. 7/2024 (ICT technical-approval guideline); MyGIFOSS (open-source software) framework. Reference: PK 2.1 para 2.10.
4. **No mandatory MySEAL or national-crypto requirement written into tender conditions.** PK specifications refer to Malaysian or international standards (PK 2.1 paras 5.12–5.16), and MyGPKI uses FIPS, PKCS and X.509. Reference: PK 2.1 para 5.
5. **No ownership or control condition on cloud providers supplying government.** PKPA 1/2021 defines a CSP as "dalam negara mahupun luar negara" (domestic or foreign). The only local element is the MSP (R35). Reference: PK 2.6.
6. **No binding Treasury circular implementing the 13th Malaysia Plan (RMK-13, 2026–2030) or PuTERA35 in procurement.** No PK title or text mentions them. The Bumiputera measures in force are PK 1.5 (amended 1 Jan 2025) and the 2026 circular PK 2.21. Reference: PK 1.5.
7. **No statutory procurement code in force.** Act 882 has not commenced (R14), so AP and PP/PK remain the operative rules. Reference: FPA s. 4 and AP 168.
8. **The open-source policy is not a procurement preference by nationality.** The MyGIFOSS "Preferences and Guidelines" are technology choices, not supplier nationality. Reference: PK 2.1 para 5.15 (ICT specifications follow JDN policy).
9. **Telecom local content (JENDELA / universal service, Digital Nasional Berhad) is not government procurement for own use.** The JENDELA invitation contains no local-content clause, and DNB is a GLC. See R17. Reference: PK 1.1 para 4.1 (definition of government procurement) and para 13.1.

## 7. Indicator summary: proposed score vs held gold

**2.1 Foreign exclusions: proposed 1 · gold 1 (agree on the score, disagree on the basis).**
- *Rows it rests on:* R1 (PK 2.2 para 2.1, the Category A/C rule), R2 (PK 1.4 para 2.5(i)), R3 (PK 2.1 para 3.1(ii)(j)), R4 (PK 7.6 para 1.2(viii), ICT-specific), R5 (PK 2.9 paras 1.3 and 1.7, local-only quotation band), R6, R7 and R8 (Bumiputera-only categories), and R9 (a 2026 amendment).
- *Trap reasoning:* each is either an outright category bar or "foreign only when local unavailable or lacking expertise". Under trap step 1 and the ESCAP FAQ, that scores 1.
- *Disagreement with gold:* the gold scores **FPA 1957** at 1. Under the legal-basis trap, FPA s. 4 (Treasury instructions), s. 6 (the Minister's financial control) and s. 36 (Financial Regulations) only empower, so they score **0**. The exclusion lives in the PK circulars, chiefly PK 2.2 para 2.1 and PK 1.4 para 2.5(i).
- The gold's second 2.1 row ("Treasury Directive 1966", score 0) is really AP (Pindaan 2023). It *does* contain an exclusion: AP 169.2(a), as implemented by PK 1.4 and PK 2.1.
- The gold's s. 36 description ("may provide… preferences or exclusions for… ICT") is not in the text. s. 36 authorises general Financial Regulations only.

**Where AP 169.2(a) belongs: 2.1, not 2.3.** Its structure is "imports [and foreign services] considered only after it is ensured that local goods and services cannot be obtained". There is no path for a foreign product or service to win while a local one is available, which is exactly the ESCAP-FAQ exclusion pattern scored 1 under 2.1 (trap step 1). PK 1.4 para 2.5(i) makes the foreign-services limb explicit, and PK 7.6 para 1.2(viii) applies it to software.
- For goods, one could argue it is an origin-based local-content rule (2.3). But a rule that eliminates imports rather than weighting them is an exclusion, not a limitation.
- The gold's other 2.3 items are fine as 2.3: AP 169B(a) and 184A(a) are cost differentials (184A(a) is [NON-ICT-SECTOR] works). AP 190B and AP 287(a) are neutral: registration and technical approval.

**2.2 Source code, encryption, trade secrets: proposed 1 (Medium confidence; strict alternative 0) · gold 0 (disagree).**
- *Rows it rests on:* R20 (PK 2.2 Lampiran 2 clause (xvii)(a)), a standard tender condition that vests "all copyright and other IP rights in goods supplied" in the Government. It is not limited to bespoke work, so it amounts to surrender of exclusive IP as a condition of winning. Corroborated by R19 (PK 2.1 para 2.10(iv): mandatory government ownership of source code and IPR for ICT application development, [BESPOKE-DELIVERABLE]) and R21 (PK 2.8, RFP proposals usable without compensation whether or not the bidder wins).
- *Trap reasoning:* the clauses are conditions set by the Treasury, not "subject to agreement" terms, so the "optional clause" exception does not apply.
- On a strict reading (R20 read as a bespoke-only template, R19 bespoke, R21 not source code), the score would be **0**. The deciding factor is consistency with how RDTII treats Indonesia's PP 71/2019 Art. 9, which is also a bespoke hand-over (see §4).
- *Encryption limb:* **0.** MyKriptografi (R22, 2025) mandates national trusted crypto products for Official Secret data, but as a data-handling rule, not a tender condition ([OUT-OF-SCOPE-11.4]). MySEAL (R23) is a guideline; MyGPKI (R24) binds agencies only and uses international standards.
- The gold's OSA s. 2 row is correctly 0. The procurement-level hook is PK 2.1 para 2.11 (R25), a confidentiality condition.

**2.3 Limitations in bidding: proposed 1 · gold 1 (agree on the score, disagree on the basis).**
- *Rows it rests on:* R26 (Bumiputera price margins, 10% down to 2.5%), R27 (Bumiputera–foreign JV capped at 49% foreign), R28 (price margins by origin in covered procurement), R29 (industrial collaboration / offsets: RM50m threshold for foreign vs RM100m for local, 100% credit value), R30 (RM0.5m–1m tender deposit only for international tenderers), R31 (SSM and MOF registration only for foreign FTA firms). Each discriminates by ownership, nationality or origin (trap step 2), so each scores 1.

The Bumiputera preference and offset evidence should instead cite PK 1.5 and PK 1.7.
