## Scope and scoring treatment

I treated a measure as relevant only where it applies to private-sector or horizontally applicable data. Government-only measures are reported separately and marked **[GOV-ONLY]**. The research cut-off is **22 August 2026**. The principal horizontal regime is Federal Law No. 152-FZ, as amended through Federal Law No. 265-FZ of 26 July 2026; the official legal portal lists those amendments as part of the current legislative history.[^1][^2]

The evidence supports:

- **P6-I1:** 0 — no outright, horizontal ban on cross-border data transfers.
- **P6-I2:** 1 — horizontal personal-data localisation requires a Russian database for specified collection and primary processing operations.
- **P6-I3:** 0 — no verified horizontal rule requiring a local server or data centre as a precondition to providing a service.
- **P6-I4:** 1 — horizontal personal-data transfers are permitted conditionally, subject to statutory grounds, notification and possible restriction.
- **P6-I5:** 0 — Russia has at least one binding international data-transfer instrument: Convention 108.

## Negative findings

- **No verified outright horizontal ban:** Federal Law No. 152-FZ Art. 12 expressly allows cross-border transfers on statutory conditions. The adequacy requirement, notice requirement, review period and Roskomnadzor restriction power are conditional controls, not a universal prohibition.[^3][^4]
- **No verified P6-I3 local-infrastructure precondition:** The Russian personal-data localisation rule requires specified processing through Russian databases, but it does not say that a provider must operate a Russian server or data centre before it may offer the service. Under the rubric, the rule is P6-I2.
- **No separate P6-I3 finding from cybersecurity rules:** CII, encryption, access control, incident-response, monitoring and supervisory-access requirements were excluded because they regulate security or governmental access rather than local infrastructure.
- **No additional private-sector horizontal non-personal-data restriction verified:** The official-portal searches did not establish a general Russian requirement that non-personal data be stored domestically or that non-personal data transfers be prohibited.
- **No business-transfer finding:** Rules concerning transfer, amalgamation or restructuring of businesses, assets, shares or banking undertakings are not data-transfer restrictions.
- **Government-only measures excluded:** Government cloud, e-government, public-authority information systems, CII obligations applicable only to state bodies, and the Art. 12 exemptions implemented by Resolution No. 2526 are flagged [GOV-ONLY] and not scored.
- **Adequacy-list detail:** Roskomnadzor’s official portal confirms that transfers to non-adequate countries are subject to a ten-working-day standstill after notification, except for vital-interest cases; the complete operative adequacy-country list and all 2024–2026 amendments were not retrievable from the official portal in this research pass.[^4]
- **2026 amendment caution:** Federal Law No. 265-FZ of 26 July 2026 directly amends Art. 12. Its enactment is verified, but the full consolidated text and commencement provisions were not fully retrievable; the final database should verify whether it changes the scoring treatment before assigning any additional row.[^2]


## Could not verify on official portal

- A separate **Bank of Russia** instrument adding a new P6-I2 or P6-I4 obligation beyond the payment-data material already held.
- A distinct **insurance-sector** rule requiring all policyholder or insurance-record data to remain in Russia, beyond the instruments already excluded by the user’s skip-list.
- A **Unified Licence/ISP Licence** clause imposing a general local-server or domestic-data-centre precondition for telecom or internet service provision.
- A binding Russian **RCEP** commitment: Russia is not an RCEP party.
- A Russian **DEPA** accession or accession commitment by 22 August 2026.
- A Russian FTA/CEPA e-commerce chapter containing a binding “shall not prohibit or restrict cross-border transfer of information by electronic means” obligation.
- An official Russian instrument creating a **binding adequacy/BCR mechanism** comparable to the EU GDPR’s adequacy or binding-corporate-rules systems.
- A separate private-sector **cloud-first/local-cloud** rule meeting P6-I3 rather than a government-only or procurement condition.
