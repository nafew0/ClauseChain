# Singapore — Pillar 2 deep research (public procurement of ICT goods and digital services)

Singapore · Pillar 2 · in force as of August 2026 · researched 29 Sep 2026

Method: every main-table row was read on sso.agc.gov.sg, mof.gov.sg, gebiz.gov.sg, tech.gov.sg (including developer.tech.gov.sg and info.standards.tech.gov.sg), csa.gov.sg, imda.gov.sg or wto.org during this session. SSO was reached as HTML and PDF, for current and historical versions; one request was briefly throttled (HTTP 202/403) and succeeded on retry. I searched smartnation.gov.sg and found nothing relevant (one page returned 404).
Blocked or partial: the e-gpa.wto.org annex pages render only in the browser, so I used the WTO loose-leaf Appendix I .doc files instead. IMDA's archived 2013–2014 IDA press releases now return 404. GeBIZ tender documents require a login. MOF's Government Instruction Manual on Procurement is not published. Sources outside the brief's portal list (mti.gov.sg, enterprisesg.gov.sg, mddi.gov.sg, mindef.gov.sg) are used only as context, never for main-table rows.

All Singapore texts are English originals, so every quote is labelled [OFFICIAL-EN]. The IDs in brackets are used in the summary at the end.

## 6. Indicator summary — proposed score vs held gold

**Does the GPA-covered framework reach below-threshold and non-covered procurement? No.**
- *What the Act covers.* The GP Act (s. 4(2)–(3), s. 7(1)), the GP Order (paras 4 and 8) and GP Regulations reg 3(1) apply only to "procurement subject to the Act". That means a listed entity, buying scheduled goods or services, above the thresholds (SDR 130,000, 400,000 or 5m), from a supplier of a relevant State or Protocol State.
- *What that leaves out.* Everything else falls under MOF's unpublished Instruction Manual and agency rules, with no statutory duty and no Tribunal challenge (T-02). That includes below-threshold buys (small-value purchases up to S$6,000, quotations up to S$90,000, and smaller tenders) and unscheduled ICT services. For non-FTA partners, the unscheduled services include CPC 842 (apart from 84210), 845, 849, basic 752 and all of 754.
- *Non-discrimination there is only a practice.* It is an administrative "default practice" (WTO TPR S/413 para 3.141, "according to the authorities"), and the IMDA panels show it is not absolute.
- *Scale.* For context, TPR S/413 reports 2018 contracts above the GPA thresholds of S$6.5bn (goods and services) and S$15.8bn (construction), against S$30.0bn of total awards. Being above a threshold does not by itself mean covered by the entity and service lists.

**2.1 — proposed 0; held gold 0.**
- *Rows it rests on:* 21-01, 21-07 to 21-16; negative findings 1–4.
- *Trap reasoning:* The national-security and MHA carve-outs (21-07, 21-08) are discretions or coverage exclusions, not exclusions of foreign firms (legal-basis trap). GCC residency is Pillar 6 (cross-pillar trap). Registration (GeBIZ, GSR) is open to foreign entities.
- *Where I disagree with the gold:* I agree on the score but not the basis. The gold leans on GP Act s. 6, which is an enabling clause (legal-basis trap), so it proves neither openness nor restriction. The gold also misdescribes s. 6 as allowing "limitations on foreign participation where national interests or security concerns arise"; s. 6 contains no such words and is confined to implementing the GPA and other international obligations. The openness evidence should be reg 13(3)(a), reg 14(2), the foreign GeBIZ and GSR routes, and GCC.
- *Gold timeframes are stale:* the Order was last amended with effect from 1 Jul 2026 (from 15 Sep 2026 after the cut-off); the Regulations and Challenge Regulations are now 2025 Revised Editions.

**2.2 — proposed 0.5 (medium-low confidence); held gold 0.**
- *Rows it rests on:* 22-05 and 22-06 (NITES: CSA evaluation required for IT products handling sensitive government data, framed by CSA as a "policy requirement for government procurement" and recognised only in Singapore).
- *Trap reasoning:* All other rows score 0. Reg 6(a) requires generally available encryption for e-procurement. The IM8 crypto controls are agency-only and industry-standard. The Tender Lite IP clause is an optional [BESPOKE-DELIVERABLE]. SCCS is voluntary, and the Cybersecurity Act amendments are out of scope (CII).
- *Where I disagree with the gold:* its reasoning misreads the held provisions. GP Order para 5(3)(b) is a ground for an agency to disapply the Regulations to protect IP rights of the Government or other parties; it says nothing about source code in ICT products. Reg 6(b) concerns the integrity of e-bids, not trade secrets as a tender condition.
- *Fallback:* if NITES is not accepted as a "specific encryption" mandate, because its crypto content is unpublished, the score falls to 0, which agrees with the gold's number but not its reasoning.

**2.3 — proposed 1; held gold 0.**
- *Rows it rests on:* 23-01, 23-02 and 23-03 (IMDA Accreditation): Government buyers "must consider" the AC Panel "first", under a MOF–IMDA streamlined process, and eligibility requires Singapore registration plus substantial Singapore activity. 23-04 (IMDA Spark Government Procurement Panel) is a second channel on the same basis.
- *Trap reasoning:* Step 1: foreign suppliers can still win open tenders, so this is 2.3, not 2.1. Step 2: the advantage is tied to local establishment, so it discriminates by origin and scores 1. A single discriminatory measure is enough; the transparency limb is not needed and is not triggered (T-01). No in-force 0.5-type condition was found: the CSA-marks proposal is not adopted (23-13) and the MTCS requirement is unverified.
- *Where I disagree with the gold:* the gold relies on reg 4, which binds only covered procurement (reg 3(1)); on s. 6, an enabling clause (legal-basis trap); and on the TPR's "open sourcing" statement. In the non-covered space, where most ICT purchasing sits, MOF and IMDA run a priority channel for Singapore-established firms that the gold missed. The gold's note that "prospective bidders may be excluded if a selective tender procedure is utilised" is not a limitation: selective tendering shortlists on capability (regs 23–24).
- *Alternative reading:* a reviewer who treats the IMDA test as status-based (foreign-owned Singapore entities can qualify) would score 0.5.

**2.4 — proposed 0.5; no gold held.**
- *Rows it rests on:* 24-01 and 24-02, with 24-03 and 24-04 as context.
- *Reasoning:* Singapore is a GPA 2012 party with a positive-list Annex 5 that covers CPC 752 and CPC 84 only in part and CPC 754 not at all, so it covers at least one but not all three. FTA-extended coverage for the US, Australia, the EU, the UK and Chinese Taipei is preferential and not scored.
