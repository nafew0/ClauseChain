# RDTII 2.1 – Pillar 2 (Public procurement of ICT goods and digital services) – India: deep research

**Status date:** law in force as of **August 2026**. Research done **29 September 2026**. Official portals were checked live on that date, and later changes up to that day are flagged.

**Scope:** new findings beyond the held Pillar 2 gold rows for India (see `gold_india.md`). Held instruments appear here only as (a) a different provision, (b) a subordinate or implementing instrument, or (c) a 2023–2026 amendment, supersession or repeal (a currentness row). Where the brief's traps give a different indicator or score than the gold, the reason is in **Gold re-mapping notes**.

## Scoring summary (held gold + new findings)

**2.1 = 1.** Under PPP-MII Order 2017 (rev. 19.07.2024) cl. 3(b), only Class-I and Class-II local suppliers may bid. Non-local supply is admitted only in a Global Tender Enquiry (GTE). GFR Rule 161(iv)(b) bars GTEs below ₹200 crore unless the Department of Expenditure (DoE) approves, and a GTE may be issued only where goods "may not be available in the country". This is the "foreign only if nationals can't meet demand" pattern, which scores 1. The DoT Gazette notification of 21.10.2024 adds a category bar under cl. 3(a) for 36 telecom product categories, including encryption/UTM platforms, routers and switches: only Class-I local suppliers may bid, at any value. The land-border regime (DoE Order (Public Procurement No. 4) of 23.02.2023) is a separate 0.5 group exclusion.

**2.2 = 1 (Medium confidence).** The gazetted Policy on Collaborative Application Development by Opening the Source Code of Government Applications (06.05.2015) says procurement should ensure the Government receives the source code of, and unlimited rights to, custom-built applications. It also claims full rights to vendors' customisation code on COTS products. Read strictly under the brief's "mandatory standard-contract clause vesting source code/IP in the government" rule, this scores 1. **Fallback: 0.5.** Bidding on the CPPP needs a Class-3 DSC from a CA licensed under the IT Act s. 24. The gold's 2.2 = 1 rested on the OSS Adoption Policy, which is the wrong basis: it is a preference and requires no hand-over of code.

**2.3 = 1.** Several measures discriminate by origin or nationality:
- the 20% margin of purchase preference for Class-I local suppliers (PPP-MII cl. 3A/6);
- the MSE L1+15% price-band preference, which depends on Udyam registration (Aadhaar, PAN and GSTIN);
- the DPIIT-startup relaxations in GFR Rules 170 and 173;
- the MeitY cloud empanelment of July 2026, which requires incorporation in India, plus in-India data residency [ALSO-P6-GOV-ONLY].

Institutional transparency is present: GFR, the manuals, the eGazette and the CPPP are all public.

**2.4 = 1.** On wto.org, India is a GPA **observer since 10 February 2010**. It is not a party and is not in accession.

Weighted Pillar 2 result (40/32/20/8): **1.00** if 2.2 = 1. It is 0.84 if 2.2 falls back to 0.5.

## Gold re-mapping notes

1. **GFR Rule 153(iii) (gold: 2.1 = 1).** This is an enabling clause ("The Central Government may, by notification, provide for …"). Under the legal-basis trap it scores **0 by itself**. India's 2.1 = 1 should rest on the implementing measures: PPP-MII cl. 3(b) read with GFR Rule 161(iv)(b) (row 1), and cl. 3(a) as used in the DoT notification of 21.10.2024 (row 6). Keep 153(iii) as context.
2. **PPP-MII cl. 3 (gold: 2.1 = 1).** The score is consistent with the brief, but the pinpoint should add GFR 161(iv)(b). Eligibility turns on the **local content** of the goods or services, not the bidder's nationality. A foreign-owned firm that makes goods in India with ≥50% local content is Class-I. A stricter reading would treat the Class-I/II bar as a local-content requirement (2.3). We keep 2.1 = 1 because non-local supply is admitted only in GTEs, which are barred below ₹200 cr (the "foreign only if nationals can't meet demand" pattern). The gold also omits the 28.05.2018 and 29.05.2019 revisions from its timeline.
3. **MSE Order (gold: 2.1 = 0, while its comment says it "automatically restricts foreign firms").** This is internally inconsistent. Re-mapping:
   - the **358-item reservation** (para 11) is a category exclusion, but it is [NON-ICT-SECTOR] (row 15), so the ICT 2.1 score stays 0;
   - the **L1+15% price-band preference** (para 6) and the 25% target with SC/ST and women sub-targets are status-based and open only to **Udyam-registered** (Indian-identifier) enterprises, so 2.3 = 1 (rows 14, 16). The gold's 2.3 = 1 for the MSE Order stands, but the reason is Udyam registration rather than the target alone.
4. **Electronic products notification (gold: 2.1 = 0.5 for mobile phones under cl. 3(a)).** Under the brief, a Class-I-only bar for a whole product category is an exclusion for a category, which scores **1**. The 0.5 tier is reserved for exclusion of a specific *group of foreign firms*, such as the land-border order. The gold's companion 2.3 = 1 row (Class-I/II thresholds) is by itself an LCR worth 0.5. 2.3 = 1 is justified by the origin-based 20% purchase-preference margin (row 8) and by the several nationality-based measures together.
5. **DoT telecom PPP-MII notification of 29.08.2018 (gold: 2.3 = 1).** It is **superseded** by the Gazette notification of 21.10.2024 (row 6), which states "in supersession of … 29.08.2018 and 31.08.2021". Under the current regime, Table-A items fall under cl. 3(a) (Class-I only), which maps to **2.1 (1)**. Local-content thresholds and design-IPR conditions stay in 2.3 (row 7). This settles the gold's note "May be deleted…".
6. **OSS Adoption Policy 2015 and OSS Framework (gold: 2.2 = 1).** An OSS preference is not surrender of source code. The policy requires RFPs to ask suppliers to consider OSS and to justify excluding it (cl. 6, 8(ii)). No bidder has to hand over code of its proprietary product, so **2.2 = 0** for this instrument. At most it is a technical preference applied to all bidders (2.3 at 0.5, not nationality-based). India's 2.2 score instead rests on the **Source-Open Policy of 06.05.2015** (row 21), with the Class-3 DSC (row 23) as fallback.
7. **Policy on Open Standards cl. 4.1.2 (gold: 2.2 = 0).** Agreed. The royalty-free-patent rule is a criterion the government uses to *select* "Identified Standards". It is not a surrender by bidders. IT Act s. 84A is an enabling clause with no rules made (see Negative findings), so 0 [OUT-OF-SCOPE-11.4].
8. **MeitY Cloud 2017 documents (gold: 2.3 = 1).** The 2.3 = 1 score holds, but the 2017 instruments have been replaced by the empanelment invitation (Aug 2025 / July 2026) and the Cloud 2.0 documents of 18.03.2026. The current basis is Indian incorporation (Annexure-6(i)), which is nationality-based, plus data residency and the Indian-law requirement (§5.2.4). Add the flag [ALSO-P6-GOV-ONLY]. The gold comment also mis-dates the 2015 guidelines.
9. **PMA Notification 2013 and Guidelines 2015 (gold: 2.3 = 1).** Currentness is doubtful. The gold itself notes the 2013 notification "will be in operation for 10 years" (so to about Dec 2023). PPP-MII cl. 19 requires older local-content orders to be realigned, and MeitY's 07.09.2020 electronics notification reportedly supersedes the earlier electronic-product notifications (secondary source; see "Could not verify"). Treat PMA as **lapsed or superseded**, not as an August 2026 in-force measure.
10. **Land-border regime.** The gold holds no row for it. Order (PP) No. 1 of 2020 was superseded on 23.02.2023 by Order (PP) No. 4 (rows 4–5), which is the operative instrument.

## Negative findings

Reference provisions: GFR 2017 Chapter 6 (Rules 144–176) and the PPP-MII Order 2017 (rev. 19.07.2024).

- **No MeitY PPP-MII notification for software products or IT services** was found. Software is covered only by the PPP-MII defaults (50%/20%; DPIIT FAQ Q.8). DoT's review notice of 03.06.2025 lists "Criteria for calculating Local content for Software Products" as still to be settled. Reference: PPP-MII cl. 5.
- **No 2025–26 revision of the PPP-MII Order** appears on the TEC PPP-MII circulars list or the DPIIT pages (latest: 19.07.2024). **No replacement of the DoT notification of 21.10.2024** was found; only review notices of 06.04, 04.06 and 13.06.2025.
- **No 2024–26 amendment of the land-border regime.** The DoE Procurement Policy Division list (entries to 10.09.2026) shows none. The Jan 2026 reports of a plan to scrap the curbs have not produced an order. The Mar 2026 easing concerned FDI (Press Note 2 of 2026) [OUT-OF-SCOPE-3.x]. Reference: GFR Rule 144(xi).
- **No rules under IT Act s. 84A** (modes or methods of encryption) were found. The 2015 draft National Encryption Policy was withdrawn. No procurement rule mandates a national encryption algorithm in products bought [OUT-OF-SCOPE-11.4]. Reference: GFR Rule 173(i) and the bidding-document content rules.
- **No mandatory source-code escrow.** The Model RFP (2018) treats escrow as "Optional" (§2.16; see "Could not verify"), and GFR and the DoE manuals contain none. Reference: GFR Chapter 6.
- **No outright nationality bar in GFR.** Foreign principals may quote through enlisted Indian agents (GFR Rule 152). Restrictions work through local content (PPP-MII), the land-border order and registration-based preferences. Reference: GFR Rules 144, 152.
- **No government-only ban on a foreign app, software or cloud vendor** written into procurement rules was found. The s. 69A app-blocking orders are general blocking orders, not procurement measures ([ALSO-10.1] not triggered). Reference: GFR Rule 144.
- **No set-aside for medium enterprises** and **no foreign quota** were found. Reference: MSE Order 2012 para 3.
