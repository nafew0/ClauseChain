# Mongolia — RDTII 2.1 Pillar 2 (Public procurement of ICT goods and digital services): deep research

**Status:** in force as of August 2026. Research done 29 September 2026. Primary portal: legalinfo.mn (Unified Legal Information System). Every legalinfo.mn URL below was opened and read on 29 Sep 2026.

**Language:** quotes are my own translations from the Mongolian Cyrillic text, labelled **[OWN-TRANSLATION]**. For the Procurement Law, I cross-checked them against the unofficial English translation embedded on the same legalinfo.mn page (dated 26.10.2023, which is the pre-amendment text).

## Scoring summary

These scores combine the held gold with the new findings. With weights 40/32/20/8, they give 68 of 100 points.

| Indicator | Proposed score | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| **2.1 (foreign exclusion)** | **1** | Held gold: Art. 8.1 (buy domestic where domestic production can supply) with Government Resolution No. 90. New evidence shows the exclusion is operational:<br>• Art. 26.10.2 of the 2023 Procurement Law rejects imported goods offered in a domestic-goods lot. Art. 10.4 requires those goods to be lotted separately.<br>• Art. 18.3.7 requires every notice to state whether foreign persons may take part. Read with Art. 8.3, a procuring entity may close tenders below MNT 1 bn (goods) and MNT 300 m (services) to foreign persons.<br>• The MoF standard bidding documents of 26.05.2025 turn this into a data-sheet switch ("Эрхтэй/Эрхгүй", entitled / not entitled). The MoF evaluation methodology of 25.12.2023 tells evaluators to reject foreign bids in closed tenders or lots.<br>This is the ESCAP FAQ "foreign only if nationals can't supply" pattern plus a category exclusion, so it scores 1. |
| **2.2 (source code / encryption)** | **0** (borderline) | Several instruments now deal with IP and source code in procurement: the Law on Supporting IT Production 2024 Art. 7.4, Procurement Law Art. 11.9, and the MoF services SBD (ITB 1.5 and GCC 3.16–3.17). None is a mandatory surrender:<br>• Art. 7.4 lets source code go to the customer **or the contractor** "as mutually agreed". That is a negotiated clause, so it scores 0 under the 2.2 condition trap.<br>• The foreground-IP vesting in the services SBD is a default that the procuring entity may switch off.<br>• No certified or national cryptography is a tender condition. The Cyber Security Law 2021 binds only agencies (→ 11.4). The bidder digital-signature rules are about authenticating bids, not a product-encryption condition.<br>The gold's 0 stands, but for different reasons (see Gold re-mapping notes). |
| **2.3 (limitations)** | **1** | Held gold: Art. 8.4–8.7 origin-based margins of 10% / 15% / 7.5%, where the 7.5% requires a Mongolian-citizen ultimate beneficial owner. New findings:<br>• The 15% SME tier is nationality-based. The SME Law (Art. 3.1–3.2) covers only domestic entities registered and permanently operating in Mongolia and excludes foreign legal entities.<br>• The MoF methodology A/255 (§8, Form 6) gives the calculation mechanics.<br>• The goods SBD ITB 17.8 imposes an after-sales local-presence condition on foreign bidders only.<br>• Software counts as "goods" under Art. 4.1.4, so the domestic-goods margins reach off-the-shelf software.<br>Transparency is adequate, so the transparency limb is not triggered. |
| **2.4 (GPA)** | **1** | Mongolia is a GPA **observer since 23 February 1999**, not a party. It is not listed as negotiating accession. Its 1996 accession commitment (WT/ACC/MNG/9 para. 59) was to seek observer status and to open membership negotiations "thereafter", and this remains unfulfilled. It has no coverage schedule, so there is no CPC 752/754/84 coverage. |

## Gold re-mapping notes

1. **2.1: the Art. 8.3 mechanism is described backwards in the gold.**
   - The gold says tenders above the values "require an international open tender to be announced mandatorily". The Procurement Law says no such thing. Art. 8.3 only forbids barring foreign persons above MNT 30 bn / 1 bn / 300 m, and Art. 11.3 makes an international language optional.
   - The restrictive content is the converse: below those values the procuring entity may close the tender to foreigners. Arts. 18.3.7 and 26.10, the SBD data-sheet switch and the A/255 checklist implement this.
   - Under trap Step 1 this is a category exclusion, so the score of 1 is unchanged but the mechanism should be re-described.
2. **2.1: the ICT nexus of Resolution No. 90 is weaker than the gold implies.**
   - Item 14.9 (electrical connection equipment) is electrical, not ICT. The only ICT-adjacent items are 11.6 (printer and photo toner) and 13.5 (copper and signal cables).
   - Res. 90 was issued under repealed-law Art. 10¹.2 (legalinfo prints it as "101.2"). It remains in force, amended by Res. 381/2022 and Res. 113/2023, but no list has been re-approved under the 2023 law's Art. 8.2.
   - The 2.1 = 1 score should rest mainly on the horizontal Art. 8.1 / 8.3 / 18.3.7 / 26.10.2 package, which reaches ICT goods and services, rather than on the list's content.
3. **2.3: the gold mixes exclusion and limitation, and cites a non-measure.**
   - Art. 8.1 (held in both 2.1 and 2.3) belongs in 2.1 only. Double-counting it in 2.3 breaks the 2.1/2.3 trap.
   - Arts. 8.8 and 4.1.8 are not restrictions: 8.8 switches preferences off for framework (general-contract) purchases. They should be context, not scoring evidence.
   - The 2.3 = 1 score is still correct. It rests on origin-based margins (8.4–8.6 plus A/255), the nationality-based SME tier (SME Law Art. 3.2) and the Mongolian-citizen ultimate beneficial owner margin in works (8.7.2).
4. **2.3: correct the gloss of Art. 8.4.4.** The gold's note calls it "products produced in innovation park". Art. 8.4.4 refers to "innovative products" as defined in Innovation Law Art. 3.1.4, a general definition with no park requirement.
5. **2.2: the score of 0 stands, but the gold's reasons are out of date.**
   - The gold says Mongolia "does not demand" source code. Since June 2024 the Law on Supporting IT Production (Art. 7.4) requires every software tender to state source-code ownership. Separately, PL Art. 11.9 and the services SBD vest foreground IP in the customer by default.
   - The score is 0 only because these clauses are negotiable or can be switched off (2.2 condition trap).
   - The **Law on Organization Secret (1995)** that the gold cites has no bearing on procurement conditions. The reference rows should be PL 11.9, IT Law 7.4, the Cyber Security Law and A/249.
6. **Timeframe fix.** The gold's "Since June 2023" (2.2 and 2.3) should be **"Since 1 January 2024"**. The law was adopted 16.06.2023 and entered into force 01.01.2024 under Art. 62.1 as amended 10.11.2023.

## Negative findings

Each negative finding uses the Procurement Law as its reference row: https://legalinfo.mn/mn/detail?lawId=16760359992351.

- **No SME set-aside, quota or reserved share in procurement.** Reference: PL Art. 8.6, whose only SME benefit is the 15% margin. The SME Law sets only a policy direction to "support through public procurement" (Art. 8.1.5).
- **No mandatory consortium, national-partner or local-subcontracting requirement.** Reference: PL Art. 6. The only nationality-linked incentive is held Art. 8.7.2 (works).
- **No certified or national cryptography, and no specific encryption algorithm, as a condition of bidding or winning.** Reference: PL Arts. 11–13 (technical specifications). Art. 12.4 forbids naming a brand, patent or origin in specifications.
  - The Cyber Security Law 2021, the E-Signature Law 2021 and MoF A/248 contain no such tender condition.
  - The Law on State and Official Secrets was not reviewed. State-secret and national-security procurement is carved out of the PL (Art. 3.3.1, rules under Art. 3.4) → [NON-ICT-SECTOR] defence.
- **No mandatory source-code escrow or deposit in a state fund.** Reference: PL Art. 11.9. IT Law Art. 7.4 is negotiated, and Art. 7.5 concerns state-owned code only.
- **No government-cloud or National Data Center mandate written into tender eligibility.** Reference: PL Art. 15.4, which prohibits requirements unrelated to contract performance. The residency rules in Law on Public Information Transparency Arts. 18.12, 27.7 and 33 are P6 [GOV-ONLY].
- **No 2023–2026 replacement of Resolution No. 90 under PL Art. 8.2, and no ICT goods on any domestic-goods list.** Reference: PL Arts. 8.1–8.2. Res. 133/2026 lists no ICT items.
- **No implementing instrument for the innovation-law procurement preference beyond PL Art. 8.4.4.** Reference: PL Art. 8.4.4.
- **Recent IT-sector support instruments are tax and non-tax subsidies, not procurement preferences.** Reference: PL Art. 8.
  - Res. 39/2023: support for start-ups and software firms.
  - Res. 98/2025: IT-production virtual zone.
  - Res. 290/2026: list of IT products, works and services of virtual-zone entities, issued under Corporate Income Tax Law Art. 22.5.4.
  - These are [OUT-OF-SCOPE-10.x], not 2.3.
- **Res. 163/2024 (centralised framework purchase of document-software licences) is neutral.** Reference: PL Art. 10.7. It carries no origin or nationality condition.
- **No amendment in 2024–2026 changed Art. 8 or the Art. 8.3 values.** Reference: PL Art. 8.
- **No government-procurement chapter was identified in the EAEU–Mongolia Interim Trade Agreement.** See the [PREFERENTIAL-CONTEXT] section.
